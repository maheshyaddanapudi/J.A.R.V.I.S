Every simulated day of the run in one fixed format: what the memory grew by, what changed, what was asked back and what it knew. Same rows the harness writes, nothing summarised away.

Batteries

One row per quiz. Old world is the first act's 353 facts (some over 600 days old when asked); chapter two the facts that arrived after day 540. Honest misses are "not found" answers; wrong are a stale or neighbouring value.

Daily ledger

Every day. Δ is against the previous day. Night: sleep = the ordinary nightly cycle, lab = a Night Lab experiment ran, quiet = a quiet fortnight day. Quiz days are tinted.

Reading notes

  • What "recall" means. A hit is the current announced value appearing in the model's numbered answer line, and the line not saying "not found". The scorer is lenient about phrasing and strict about the value.
  • Most old-world misses are old. A handful of first-act facts never landed in memory in the first place (the agent skipped a statement or wrote it to a wrong home before the D-0080 fixes); they return as honest "not found" every time they are sampled. The second-act kernel cannot retroactively store them.
  • Day 500 → 501 is a kernel change, not a world change. The same database continued; the code that reads, corrects and learns was upgraded (D-0080). Compare the batteries either side.
  • Days 540–581 ran twice. Attempt 1 lost its provider credits at day 540 (four void days rolled back by verified replay) and was then stopped at day 581 when the audit showed a preference-selector defect in the fix under test. It is preserved separately; this ledger is attempt 2, restarted from the day-500 snapshot on the corrected build.
  • Wall-clock freezes cost time, never data. The container idled twice; every resume continued from the checkpoint. A day is only committed once its model calls succeeded.
